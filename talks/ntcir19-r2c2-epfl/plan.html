Navigation and motion grammar

A rule every slide follows. The keys (or a clicker) drive the talk; a slide whose overview is made of regions may also be clicked (zones, below).

  • A number on every slide, and on every step (the author, 5 November 2026: "Their should be a normalization between slide number and animation number, e.g. why the first animation of slide 2 is slide 2, while the first animation of slide 3 is slide 3.1. Also, slide number and animation number should always writen on the bottom/right, e.g. instead of having 2 when in 2.2, I prefer to see 2.2"). One rule for every slide: the overview (its entry and its rest) is "N"; attention step k is "N.k". The full position sits in the bottom-right corner of the stage, in every still, video, live and full-screen view, so a question at the end can name it ("slide 9", "3.4"); the URL's hash is the same name (#/3, #/3.4) and opens it. The cause of the old mismatch: slide 2's first use case (the support desk, with its five rounds) was the overview itself, its loop, while slide 3's first part (the corpus) was a step; so slide 2's first attention move read "2" and slide 3's read "3.1". Slide 2's overview is now still and the support desk is its step 2.1 (2.2 the contract lookup, 2.3 the knowledge base); the corner used to show only the slide's number.
  • Canvases, not cuts. Slides that share a picture are views of one canvas, and going to the next is a camera move within it, so each part is seen to belong to the whole. A camera move into the next slide is possible only when the slide before has no steps (a slide with steps is left by the fade to paper, "Forward plays, backward rests" rule 0), so slides 2 and 3 are no longer one move (5 November 2026). Three canvases: task (slides 2-3, now each standing alone), system (4-7, 9, 11: corpus at left, the pipeline in the middle, the passes below it, the run recipes to its right, the revised rules beside them), scores (8, 10). Slides 1 and 12 stand alone.
  • Every concept enters by layer (the author, 2 November 2026; A.enter in anim.js), never by rising from below: (1) its frame pops like a balloon; (2) in reading order, all of its text is typed or turned in, never shown at once: words a person wrote (a question, a passage) are typed on a keyboard at a person's pace, with now and then a typo that is noticed, erased and retyped; words the AI generated (an answer) are preceded by a small loading ring turning where they will start, the AI thinking, then typed the same way; presented text (the "Q" and "A" of the cards, a band's title and note, the handle "jknafou") is keyed in twice as fast and without a typo; a question type's name and a run's name (BITEM-PG-1), and nothing else, turn in on a split-flap board, as a station's departures, made of the label itself: each letter a flap of the label's own ground carrying its own letter in its own face and ink at its own place, all starting blank and turning together through the same alphabet, the upper half falling forward over the fold and the lower half of the next swinging down, each stopping at its own letter (so in "simple" the i lands before the s), the fold fading as it lands so the last frame is the still; numbers (a count, a rank) grow from 0; its lines are drawn by a pen; (3) marker bands are swept; (4) gauges hunt up and down before they set, their number counting with them; (5) hand marks (the green tick, the red cross) are written last. Nested concepts (the tiles of a corpus) pop with their parent; a list instead has its box drawn by hand first, then each row enters in turn by its own layers (its passage typed), then its ranks count up. A composition (the pipeline) is built in reading order, each part entering by its own layers, every arrow after both of its ends, parts of one kind together or in a ripple (the tools, the two gates). A number that counts things written in (a slab's entries) counts in step with them. Every card says what it is by an italic letter at its top left, keyed with the frame's first words: Q a question, A an answer, P a passage, N a nugget, T a model turn (the author, 3 November 2026: "it should be clear when a card is a passage or something else"); a ledger row, a tile or a tool carries none. A score a model gave (an entailment) shows it comes from a model, as an answer does: the model's name, a thinking ring, then a gauge that hunts and settles with its number counting, then its verdict written last, a green tick (accepted) or a red cross (rejected) (the author, 3 November 2026).
  • A slide is an overview, then steps of attention (the author, 4 November 2026: "I want to present the slides as a whole when changing slide so people can start reading, then I want to speak about the first item and draw attention to it, then the second and third [...] It should always be a, b or/and c"). Arriving on a slide, its entry brings the whole design in. Then each step of attention (↓ then →, or the linear next, "Two levels" below) is on one part, in reading order, until every part has had one; only then does the next press go to the next slide. A step uses (a) opacity (the focus fully opaque, the rest partly transparent), (b) animation (only the focus moves: its ball rolls, its loop plays) and/or (c) zoom (the camera closes on it), alone or together. Each slide below lists its steps; ← goes back one step, at rest.
  • Reuse the picture's own elements; add no boxes (the author, 5 November 2026: "In general, it would be great to keep using elements of the pipeline instead of adding boxes, e.g. 4.1 add a box with the question, while I think that an animation modifying the question box would be much more elegant"). A step shows what happens by animating the parts already drawn: a box fills in, grows or changes its words (slide 4.1: the pipeline's ruled question box becomes Han Solo's question), a tile lights or turns green, a message rides along the link it travels on (slides 4.2 and 4.9: the orchestrator's note to the model, folded into the ball, goes along l-type or up the dashed arrows). A new card is drawn only for a thing the picture has no place for (a turn, a passage read, a nugget, the orchestrator's note), and then it comes out of the element that makes it. A check is a fine mark: a small pill with a thin green tick pinned at the top-right corner of what it validates (concepts/check_pill.py), never a large tick over its content; the same on every checked passage or tile of the deck. Nothing important under the footer band: a zoomed step frames its content in the upper 770/900 of the frame (the shell's paper gradient covers the rest).
  • Two levels (the author, 5 November 2026: "I feel there should be a navigation by level <- -> for the slide 1,2,3... and then top down for the level so if I want to go to 2.2, I go to 2, then down then -> ->, or I go to 2 and then click on the zone I want when applicable, e.g. for the slide 3, I will be clicking on comparison and comparison question type and slide 3.4 will start animating. This later, clickable link does not apply every time, e.g. it wouldn't be possible to select 2.2 while being in 2."; shell/deck.js, anim/steps.js). The deck has a slide level and a step level:

0. *Two modes* (the author, 5 November 2026: "The presentation mode should be the default navigation : 1 -> 2 -> 2.1 -> 2.2.. -> 3 -> 3.1... to get out of the presentation mode, up or down arrow, to come back to presentation mode "P""). Presentation mode is the default, on every opening and reload (the mode is not kept: a reload always starts presenting): ← and → are the linear order through every position, 1 → 2 → 2.1 → 2.2 → 2.3 → 3 → 3.1 …, exactly as PageDown and PageUp (forward plays, backward rests; the fade to paper only between slides, the glide back from N.1 to N). ↑ or ↓ leaves it for navigation mode, and that press does its navigation move at once (↓ on an overview into N.1, ↑ on a step back to N; a bump switches all the same). In navigation mode ← → move by level (1-2 below), and a faint italic "nav" sits just above the corner number (to its left is the corner unit), which keeps its place; nothing shows in presentation mode. P returns to presentation mode from any state, without moving or playing anything (the part "Our pipeline" is now on I, P being taken). ?nav=1 opens in navigation mode, for testing. The linear keys (3) and the zones (4) work the same in both modes, and a click never changes the mode. 1. *Slide level* (navigation mode; an overview, "N"): ← → go to the previous / next slide's overview, forward on its entry, backward on its rest, by the fade to paper. ↓ enters the step level at N.1, playing step 1's enter; on a slide without steps ↓ does nothing (a bump), and ↑ on an overview does nothing. 2. *Step level* (navigation mode; "N.k"): → plays N.k+1; → past the last step leaves to the next slide's overview (its entry plays), so a talk given with → alone still flows. ← lands at N.k-1's rest (the 0.35 s fade). ← on N.1 and ↑ on any step both return to the overview N at rest: they are one move, the way back up, and do the same thing. On a live slide that move is a GLIDE: the camera and the dimming are undone smoothly in 0.5 s over the picture shown (the step's rest), then the overview's content is set (on slide 3 the filled cards return to ruled once the camera has opened); where there is nothing to undo (slide 2, whose steps transform its row) and on a video slide, it is the ordinary 0.35 s cross-fade to the rest. 3. *Linear keys* (a clicker): Space, PageDown and Enter go to the next position in order (the overview, its steps, the next slide's overview); PageUp and Backspace to the previous one, at rest (from an overview, the previous slide's last step). Home, End, T and the part keys land on an overview. Every rule of "Forward plays, backward rests" holds for every key: a press mid-motion stops it and moves, a burst is one move. 4. *Zones*: a slide whose steps are regions of its overview declares them in its motion file, zones: {k: part} (a data-a name), and only those slides are clickable. On its overview and on any of its steps, hovering a zone shows a pointer and a faint outline (the ink at 22 %, under the marks); a click plays that step's enter from the picture shown: the enter runs as always while the camera and the dimming it starts from are carried from where they were, eased out over its first 0.7 s, so the camera travels from the current view to the step's part with no fade within the slide (what else differs, a card already filled or not, is set at once at the start). Clicking the zone of the step shown acts it once more. Where zones nest, the smallest under the pointer wins. A video slide (validated) fades to the enter's first frame instead. Clicks anywhere else do nothing. Slide 3's zones: the corpus → 3.1, the six question-type cards with their tags (simple 3.2, simple w/ condition 3.3, comparison 3.4, aggregation 3.5, post-processing-heavy 3.6, multi-hop 3.7), the passage list PR → 3.8, the answer card AC → 3.9, the nuggets → 3.10. Slide 2 (transformations, not regions) and slide 1 have none. tools/walk.mjs walks every position with → and ← in presentation mode (the default), and checks on every slide that an opening is in presentation mode, → and ← are linear there, ↓ and ↑ (a bump included) switch to navigation mode doing their move, and P returns in place with nothing played; then, in navigation mode (?nav=1): the corner number and the hash at every position, ↑ and ↓ at both levels (bumps included), ↓ then each → through the steps, ↑ and ← back to the overview at rest with nothing played, → past the last step onto the next slide's entry, ← between overviews, Space, Enter and Backspace, and on slide 3 a hover and a click on every zone, from the overview and from wherever the last click landed, forward then backward, each landing on its step with only its enter played and the mode kept (and one click in presentation mode); the fade-to-paper check runs for → past the last step, PageUp and presentation mode's ← back to it, and navigation mode's ← back to the overview.

  • A slide animates by groups in layers: a layer waits for the one before it, the groups of a layer start together, all the way down (the author, 4 November 2026: "it does not stop mid animation to see another animation [...] we won't start and stop group of animated concepts mid to start another one"; 5 November 2026, on slide 4: "the animation start all the groups at the same time, when starting a group which contains group, each group should start at the same time in that same group and so on"; and on the title: "the pipeline as three group (query, model, answer) in the model there are different groups that should start simultaneously (tool calls and passages) in the tool calls there are different groups that should starts simultaneously too. The idea is simple for all the slides, first assign nested groups for each slide, then normally, for a given layer all the nested groups should start their animation simultaneously, only layer should wait for one to finish, e.g. answer waits that the model finish, the model that the query finish"). Every slide's entry is a TREE of groups, and every step's enter that has several parts is a small tree too. (1) A GROUP is a sequence of LAYERS; a layer starts the moment the layer before it in the same group has ended (its last member ended). (2) Within a layer, all its members start at the same instant, and a member that is itself a group starts its own first layer then, and so on recursively down to the leaves; a leaf is one concept entering by its layers (or one run of them). (3) Each member runs continuously to its own end, never paused for another; a short one simply ends sooner, and the next layer waits for the longest. (4) A link (an arrow, every arrow after both of its ends) belongs to the smallest group holding both its ends. Between two members that come one after the other, it is a group of its own, a LAYER BETWEEN THEM: the second waits for the arrow, the arrow for the first (the author, 5 November 2026: "the title animation forgets that the arrows are independent groups that needs to be animated between the sequence of question/model/answer"). Between members that start in the same layer, the links are drawn after them, as the group's next layer. Along a flow, the members and their arrows are sequential layers; only parallel branches share a layer (the author, 5 November 2026: "be careful, nested group animation must take into account the sequentiality, in a flat pipeline like the slide 2, the animations are "question" then "->" then "AI" then "->", ..."): slide 2's row is question → arrow → AI → arrow → answer → arrow → gate → the fork (its two branches together, each its arrow → its word → its outcome); slide 4's pipeline is read the same way, its two parallel ways (to the passages DB, to the orchestrator) sharing one layer. Blocks that are not joined by a flow (the two sides of slide 3, the corpus and the questions) are parallel and start together. (5) An idle starts when its own group ends (next rule), and at the end of the entry every idle is at its rest, the state the slide's loop starts from. (6) The slide's top level is one layer: all its groups start at t = 0 (as the author validated for the title on 5 November 2026: header, foot and emblem together). E.g. the title: header ‖ HES-SO ‖ corner unit ‖ emblem, the emblem in layers query → arrow → model → arrow → answer, the model in layers [tool calls ‖ passages ‖ the block's frame] → the calls (the bars growing ‖ the passages rolling), the tool calls one layer: their label ‖ the five tools, each its track ‖ its chip; slide 4: aside → [header ‖ pipeline], the pipeline in layers [entrance ‖ passages DB ‖ model ‖ orchestrator ‖ answer] → the links between them, each of the five its boxes together → its own links. It is declarative in anim/steps.js: A.groups(tree), where a list [a, b, ...] is one layer (its members start together), {layers: [L1, L2, ...]} a group of layers (each waiting for the one before), {group: L, then: links} the same as {layers: [L, links]}, {track, idle} a leaf that settles into its idle as it ends, and any node may carry a name. Every slide states its tree, layers explicit, in its header. The track carries its schedule (Live.info() groups, stepGroups) and measures it on its own timeline (Live.groups(k, s)), and tools/test_groups.mjs (run by build.py --check) fails on any layer whose members do not share a start or that does not start at the end of the layer before; with --strip DIR it also writes each entry's frame strip, every frame captioned with the groups running.
  • An idle starts when its own group ends (the author, 4 November 2026: "the blinking should start after finishing writing jknafou"): a resting motion (a caret blinking, a gauge breathing, a carousel rolling) begins the moment its group is done and keeps going while later groups play, not only once the whole slide has entered; the slide's loop then continues it.
  • A slide has a header band and a foot line (the author, 4 November 2026): the title starts high, under the top margin; a secondary figure beside a heading aligns its top with the heading's dividing rule and ends above the foot line (the baseline the HES-SO mark and the corner unit share), never reaching into the foot. An illustration is no bigger than it needs to be.
  • A scene acts by the same rules (the author, 3 November 2026: "animation must be thought a bit through with coherent rules and consistency"; anim.js A.act, concepts/scene.py):

1. *Nothing shows before its moment.* Every part is invisible until its own beat; nothing floats in early or sits waiting. 2. *A thing appears by popping* (people, objects, places), once, where it stays; *it travels* only when the story moves it (move, throw, leave, fall). 3. *Words are keyed in*, quickly and without a typo: names, labels, film titles, the words in a bubble, a fact on the yellow. A scene never types slowly and never shows a sentence at once. 4. *A number that counts something grows from 0* to its value (8604 days, 5 films), in step with what it counts; a year or a date is a name and is keyed in (2015, 2000). 5. *A line goes from one place to the other*: drawn from its start to its end, dashed or not (the arc from one premiere to the other, "died at" from Han to Ilum, a time line from left to right). Never scaled into place. A lit blade or beam is not a line but a light: it ignites, growing out of its hilt or its crater. 6. *A speaker before the speech*: a bubble pops only after the person or thing it belongs to is there, then its words are keyed in; a label after what it names; a fact's yellow band sweeps in, then its words. *A fact already written in the drawing is highlighted where it is* (a slate's title, a cast line, a poster's name): the band sweeps behind those words, and they are not written again under the scene (the author, 3 November 2026: "when something is written and must be highlighted, it would make more sense to just highlight what is written in the image instead of writing it again"). 7. *Story order follows the words*: inside a card each beat waits for its word to be typed; a question's scene shows what it asks about and asks with a "?", never the answer. 8. *A card and its scene run together*: the card's marker bands and gauge go on while its scene plays, and the card's verdict (a tick, a cross, a dashed "turned back" outline) is written last, after both; an answer's scene starts once the answer is written. 9. *A scene ends on its own idle, never on a replay* (the author, 5 November 2026: "similarly to the jknafou "|" blinking at the end, each of the scene concept must have an ending, instead of relooping the whole animation [...] I want all the scenes to have that normalized end of animations"; anim/idles.js, concepts/scene.py _idle and ENDS). A scene plays its action ONCE, then settles into its ENDING IDLE: a small, quiet loop on the one or two parts that carry its meaning, with at least a second of stillness between beats, every beat leaving its part exactly at rest; the action is never played again. The author's three examples are the standard: Hannibal's head tilts one way and the other, as if waiting (q_hannibal); the dashed reels are picked out one by one and the "?" rocks a little (q_pirates); the marquee's Avengers / 1917 switch plays once more, the "?" back at its end, then a still, and again (q_infinity). A question's "?" rocks now and then (_qmark), after its scene's own beat; a passage's or an answer's idle is its fact's object (a glow that swells, a blade that flickers, a count that swells, slates picked out in turn). Every scene declares its idle in its drawing (_idle(part, kind, beats, length, amount, origin): tilt, lean, bob, throb, dip, flap, gap, roam, fall), states it in words (ENDS), and scene() refuses a scene without one; an emblem (a posed disc) has none. Wherever a slide shows a scene after it acted, its idle runs: in a step that focuses it, the step's enter acts it and the step's loop is its idle (A.ending(sc, {acted: true}); re-entering the step acts it once more); in an entry, from the end of its act until its card is put away (A.idling(sc, track), the idle starting when its own group ends); on a review page, its act then its idle (A.ending(sc); build/scene-endings.html shows all fifty, and build/specimen.html plays them so). A.settle(lead, idle) is the one mechanism: the lead once, then the idle round and round for an hour, so a loop runner (t modulo its length) never comes back to the act. *Reviewed* (the author, 5 November 2026, on build/scene-endings.html: "for green lantern, I'd love to see him run back and forth while staying a bit in each side. The rest is validated."): every scene ending is validated except q_lantern's.   • One normalized order for every transition: (a) the rest of the picture is abstracted (dimmed); (b) the camera moves; (c) the target's surroundings stay abstracted; (d) the slide's own animation plays; (e) it loops, if a loop means something. 0. *Leaving a slide is one clean fade to paper* (the author, 5 November 2026: "when going from 2.2 to 3 with -> the end of animation before going to the next slide stack 2, 2.1, 2.2 which is quite ugly, the disappearance should be reworked and standardized"). Whenever the stage goes to another slide (→ from the last step, ← from the overview, a burst, Home, End, T, a part key), the stage's current frame, exactly as shown (an entry or a loop stopped where it is), fades out in 0.35 s to the empty paper; the persistent marks (the HES-SO mark, the corner unit, the slide number) stay untouched over it. Then the next slide starts from its own first frame: forward its entry, which plays; backward its rest (coming back from the next slide, its last step's rest), shown at once on the paper, its loop restarting. Nothing else is ever on the stage on the way: no overview, no other step, no two pictures cross-faded. A slide with steps can never be continued by the next one (anim/steps.js clears the next slide's CONTINUES), so continuing is only for a slide after one without steps, and only on a plain → at rest. In the shell (shell/deck.js) this is the VEIL, a paper-coloured layer over every slide layer and under the marks: it is raised over the frozen frame, the target is set under it, and it is lifted at once; a press during the fade sends it on from where it is. ?reduced=1 cuts. tools/walk.mjs checks it in both modes for every slide with steps, → from its last step and ← back: every frame until the paper is the leaving frame faded, the paper is reached, and after it only the target. Slide 3 no longer continues slide 2 (5 November 2026): its pan from slide 2's rows had nothing left to set aside once slide 2 became one transforming row, and the continued entry started on slide 2's raw markup, its three use cases drawn on top of one another. 1. *→ at rest:* the next slide's entry plays at 1×, then its rest. A slide that continues the last (one without steps) starts on its rest with no exit; any other is reached by leaving, rule 0. 2. *→ or ← while an entry plays* ("if pressing <- or -> mid animation, it should just stop the animation to move to the selected slide (we can navigate by pressing multiple times to arrow keys..)"): the entry stops where it is and the stage moves to the slide the key selects at once. Nothing is hurried to its end. 3. *A burst* (presses each within 0.5 s of the last) is one move: every press moves the destination at once, the bar shows it ("→ title"), and only once the keys have been quiet for 0.5 s does the target's entry start. No slide in between is played. 4. *Direction decides how a slide is arrived at.* Forward (→, End, a part key ahead): with its entry at 1×. Backward (←, Home, T, a part key behind): at rest, after the fade to paper of rule 0, its loop or idle restarting from its first frame. Leaving a slide is never its animation played backwards. 5. ?reduced=1 (or the OS setting) cuts between the rest stills, in both directions; nothing moves.
