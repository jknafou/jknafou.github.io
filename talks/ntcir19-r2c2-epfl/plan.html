Navigation and motion grammar

A rule every slide follows. Nothing is clickable: → and ← (or a clicker) are the only controls.

  • A number on every slide. The slide's number sits in the bottom-right corner of the stage, in every still, video and full-screen view, so a question at the end can name it ("slide 9").
  • Canvases, not cuts. Slides that share a picture are views of one canvas, and going to the next is a camera move within it, so each part is seen to belong to the whole. Three canvases: task (slides 2-3), system (4-12, 16-17, 20: corpus at left, the pipeline in the middle, the passes below it, the run recipes to its right, the revised rules beside them), scores (13-15, 18-19). Slides 1 and 21 stand alone.
  • Every concept enters by layer (the author, 2 November 2026; A.enter in anim.js), never by rising from below: (1) its frame pops like a balloon; (2) in reading order, all of its text is typed or turned in, never shown at once: words a person wrote (a question, a passage) are typed on a keyboard at a person's pace, with now and then a typo that is noticed, erased and retyped; words the AI generated (an answer) are preceded by a small loading ring turning where they will start, the AI thinking, then typed the same way; presented text (the "Q" and "A" of the cards, a band's title and note, the handle "jknafou") is keyed in twice as fast and without a typo; a question type's name and a run's name (BITEM-PG-1), and nothing else, turn in on a split-flap board, as a station's departures, made of the label itself: each letter a flap of the label's own ground carrying its own letter in its own face and ink at its own place, all starting blank and turning together through the same alphabet, the upper half falling forward over the fold and the lower half of the next swinging down, each stopping at its own letter (so in "simple" the i lands before the s), the fold fading as it lands so the last frame is the still; numbers (a count, a rank) grow from 0; its lines are drawn by a pen; (3) marker bands are swept; (4) gauges hunt up and down before they set, their number counting with them; (5) hand marks (the green tick, the red cross) are written last. Nested concepts (the tiles of a corpus) pop with their parent; a list instead has its box drawn by hand first, then each row enters in turn by its own layers (its passage typed), then its ranks count up. A composition (the pipeline) is built in reading order, each part entering by its own layers, every arrow after both of its ends, parts of one kind together or in a ripple (the tools, the two gates). A number that counts things written in (a slab's entries) counts in step with them. Every card says what it is by an italic letter at its top left, keyed with the frame's first words: Q a question, A an answer, P a passage, N a nugget, T a model turn (the author, 3 November 2026: "it should be clear when a card is a passage or something else"); a ledger row, a tile or a tool carries none. A score a model gave (an entailment) shows it comes from a model, as an answer does: the model's name, a thinking ring, then a gauge that hunts and settles with its number counting, then its verdict written last, a green tick (accepted) or a red cross (rejected) (the author, 3 November 2026).
  • A scene acts by the same rules (the author, 3 November 2026: "animation must be thought a bit through with coherent rules and consistency"; anim.js A.act, concepts/scene.py):

1. *Nothing shows before its moment.* Every part is invisible until its own beat; nothing floats in early or sits waiting. 2. *A thing appears by popping* (people, objects, places), once, where it stays; *it travels* only when the story moves it (move, throw, leave, fall). 3. *Words are keyed in*, quickly and without a typo: names, labels, film titles, the words in a bubble, a fact on the yellow. A scene never types slowly and never shows a sentence at once. 4. *A number that counts something grows from 0* to its value (8604 days, 5 films), in step with what it counts; a year or a date is a name and is keyed in (2015, 2000). 5. *A line goes from one place to the other*: drawn from its start to its end, dashed or not (the arc from one premiere to the other, "died at" from Han to Ilum, a time line from left to right). Never scaled into place. A lit blade or beam is not a line but a light: it ignites, growing out of its hilt or its crater. 6. *A speaker before the speech*: a bubble pops only after the person or thing it belongs to is there, then its words are keyed in; a label after what it names; a fact's yellow band sweeps in, then its words. 7. *Story order follows the words*: inside a card each beat waits for its word to be typed; a question's scene shows what it asks about and asks with a "?", never the answer. 8. *A card and its scene run together*: the card's marker bands and gauge go on while its scene plays, and the card's verdict (a tick, a cross, a dashed "turned back" outline) is written last, after both; an answer's scene starts once the answer is written.

  • One normalized order for every transition: (a) the rest of the picture is abstracted (dimmed); (b) the camera moves; (c) the target's surroundings stay abstracted; (d) the slide's own animation plays; (e) it loops, if a loop means something. A slide whose rest is a running process (a pipeline, a flow) loops; one that ends on a result (a ranking, a score, a graph) does not.
  • Jumps are direct. Pressing → N times goes straight to the Nth slide: the slides between are not played; one camera move zooms out a little, travels, and zooms in on the target, then (d) and (e) of the target only. Between canvases the move zooms out a little, cross-fades to the other canvas, and zooms in.
  • Back plays the move in reverse: the slide's animation unwinds, the camera returns, and the previous slide is shown at rest (its loop resumes).

This changes the deck shell (shell/deck.js, shell/deck.css, tools/build.py, tools/make_media.mjs), to be built at delivery. The shell must:

  • draw the slide number on the stage itself (not only in the bar, which ?chrome=0 hides);
  • read a CANVAS and a camera FRAME from each slide header, render each canvas once at rest, and a dimmed copy for steps (a) and (c);
  • make every camera move itself, as a CSS transform over the canvas still, so any two slides of a canvas are joined by one move; each slide's media then hold only its own step (d) and its loop (e);
  • replace the hop-by-hop travel: keys pressed in a burst set a destination, and the stage makes one direct move to it (zoom out a little, travel, zoom in), with the bar showing the destination while keys are pressed;
  • cross between canvases by zoom out, cross-fade, zoom in; play ← as the reverse of →;
  • keep ?reduced=1 as cuts between rest stills, and drop any click hook.

Journeys

The author, 3 November 2026: "a ball going from box to another through the "pipes" [...] at each box stop it should show what come outside [...] zooms in and out while building abstractions around the rest [...] the signal row should evolve until getting to the answer", and "we are going to follow han solo example from the trace, with all the tool calls/retrieval/reading/record management/entailment".

The motion grammar of a journey. The question card is typed at the pipeline's mouth, then folds into its ball (question_token), which rolls (travel) along the pipeline's own arrows from box to box. At each box it stops and the box emits (emit) what came out of it: the question type; the turn card (its reasoning, its one tool call) into the transcript; the search's 10 passages; the passages read; each nugget, its check with the model's score and verdict; the orchestrator's reply, verbatim, back up to the transcript; the gate's ruling; the answer card. The camera closes on the active box and opens again (camera); the rest of the pipeline stays abstracted (dim). After every step the signal rows in the record update (count): searches · calls of 30 · read · nuggets (turned back) · answers turned back, exactly JOURNEY's signals line. A step the slide plays slowly is full size and every layer; a step it passes quickly plays the same motions faster (pace), small, so the ball is always seen.

What becomes a card. Every passage the model or the orchestrator acted on (read, a nugget recorded or turned back, cited by a submission) is a P card with its scene (§Passage cards). A passage a search only listed is a ruled row with no words in the list the Search box emits (passage_list as its specimen 1), with its retrieval mark (dense, sparse, both); its fate is never to become a card. Han Solo's ten searches list 100 rows; 18 passages become cards.

Concepts, in order of first appearance

  • contact_handles: the GitHub, Hugging Face and LinkedIn marks, each beside "jknafou" (slide 1)
  • question_card: a question on a card, its real wording or ruled lines (slide 2)
  • flow_arrow: the ink arrow, "this leads to that"; dashed, the orchestrator's ruling back up; the pipe a ball rolls along (exists; slide 2)
  • answer_card: an answer with its confidence as number and bar, optionally marked right or wrong (slide 2)
  • type_tag: a question type's name in its own quiet colour, one of six (slide 3)
  • passage: one passage, title line and text, a span within it markable (slide 3)
  • scene: a card's gist acted out beside it, from the real text; every typed question, passage and answer gets one; a small card carries its emblem; card and scene share the card's width so no space is left (scene.fit_beside) (slide 3)
  • corpus: the corpus as a grid of passage tiles, each can light or be withheld (slide 3)
  • nugget: a claim, its quoted span, the passage it cites, the check's model, its gauge and verdict; dashed when turned back, with its reason (slide 3's callouts; on a slide first at 5)
  • passage_list: a ranked list of passages; ruled wordless rows for passages only listed (specimen 1), titled rows for those acted on, callouts with scene and nugget, slots and a stop (slide 3)
  • band: one actor's band, title and one-line note; model and orchestrator (slide 4)
  • tool_box: a step the model calls; also the indexes, the fusion and the re-ranker (slide 4)
  • gate: a double-outlined check that passes or turns back (slide 4)
  • trace_slab: one half of a pass's trace, transcript or record, filling entry by entry (slide 4)
  • pipeline: Figure 1 from the concepts above; every box a stop of the ball and a camera target (slide 4)
  • question_token: the question's ball, a small ink disc with an italic Q, into which the question card folds; what rolls through the pipes and stops at each box (slide 4)
  • turn: one model turn, reasoning then one tool call; its length drawable as a bar (slide 5)
  • signal_row: one recorded quantity, its name and value, counting as the journey goes (slide 5)
  • pass_lane: one finished pass folded into a lane under the pipeline: question, its calls, its answer; its touched tiles withheld (slide 6)
  • answer_group: matching answers gathered, with their count, votes or sum (slide 7)
  • run_file: a filed run's block for one question as it was sent (Ilum;100, its nugget lines), keyed line by line, with the organisers' mark (slide 10)
  • score_bar: a labelled bar with its value, alone, paired or grouped (slide 13)
  • confidence_strip: one bar per question, its value, some in ink (slide 14)
  • field_scatter: accuracy against HMR, one point per answer run, with iso-curves (slide 15)
  • estimator: a fitted model from recorded signals to a confidence (slide 20)
  • qr_code: a QR code with its caption, drawn as SVG (slide 21)

Motions

  • In anim.js, used as defined: enter, pop, type, think, flip, count, fill, write, act, reveal, hide (refusals: slides 10, 14, 17), draw, flow (the orchestrator's replies up, artifacts into a recipe), pulse (the loops of slides 2-4 and 20), highlight, dim (abstraction: the rest of the pipeline, withheld tiles, wordless rows, the losing group), move (re-ranked, gathered, folded into a lane, pulled out into slide 8's table), grow, settle, camera (closing on the active box and opening again); seq, par, at, stagger.
  • New, travel: the ball rolls along a pipe (a flow arrow or a chain of them) from one box to the next and stops; means "goes on to this step". Not move (re-placing) nor pulse (a bead, for loops).
  • New, emit: the box the ball stopped at puts out what it made: the output leaves the box along its out-arrow and enters there by its own layers; means "this came out of this step". One verb for every box (a tag, a turn, rows, a card, a nugget, a reply, a ruling, an answer).
  • New, pace: plays a track faster by a factor; means "passed through quickly". A combinator beside seq and par, so a quick step is the same motion as a slow one.
  • The between-slide camera move, the direct jump and the cross-canvas fade belong to the shell (see the grammar), not to anim.js.
