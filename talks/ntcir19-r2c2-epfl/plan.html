Navigation and motion grammar

A rule every slide follows. Nothing is clickable: → and ← (or a clicker) are the only controls.

  • A number on every slide. The slide's number sits in the bottom-right corner of the stage, in every still, video and full-screen view, so a question at the end can name it ("slide 9").
  • Canvases, not cuts. Slides that share a picture are views of one canvas, and going to the next is a camera move within it, so each part is seen to belong to the whole. Three canvases: task (slides 2-3), system (4-12, 16-17, 20: corpus at left, the pipeline in the middle, the passes below it, the run recipes to its right, the revised rules beside them), scores (13-15, 18-19). Slides 1 and 21 stand alone.
  • Every concept enters by layer (the author, 2 November 2026; A.enter in anim.js), never by rising from below: (1) its frame pops like a balloon; (2) in reading order, its text by kind: words a person or the system wrote (a question, an answer, a passage) are typed on a keyboard, with now and then a typo that is noticed, erased and retyped; class labels only (a question type, a run's name, a rank) turn in on a split-flap board, as a station's departures: every cell starts blank and all turn together through the same alphabet, half a letter folding over the midline at each flap, each cell stopping at its own letter (so in "simple" the i lands before the s); other fixed words (the "A" of an answer, a band's title, a grade) are part of the frame and come in with it; numbers (a count of passages or questions) grow from 0; its lines are drawn by a pen; (3) marker bands are swept; (4) gauges hunt up and down before they set, their number counting with them; (5) hand marks (the green tick, the red cross) are written last. Nested concepts (the tiles of a corpus) pop with their parent; a list instead has its box drawn by hand first, then each row pops in turn, then its ranks flip in.
  • One normalized order for every transition: (a) the rest of the picture is abstracted (dimmed); (b) the camera moves; (c) the target's surroundings stay abstracted; (d) the slide's own animation plays; (e) it loops, if a loop means something. A slide whose rest is a running process (a pipeline, a flow) loops; one that ends on a result (a ranking, a score, a graph) does not.
  • Jumps are direct. Pressing → N times goes straight to the Nth slide: the slides between are not played; one camera move zooms out a little, travels, and zooms in on the target, then (d) and (e) of the target only. Between canvases the move zooms out a little, cross-fades to the other canvas, and zooms in.
  • Back plays the move in reverse: the slide's animation unwinds, the camera returns, and the previous slide is shown at rest (its loop resumes).

This changes the deck shell (shell/deck.js, shell/deck.css, tools/build.py, tools/make_media.mjs), to be built at delivery. The shell must:

  • draw the slide number on the stage itself (not only in the bar, which ?chrome=0 hides);
  • read a CANVAS and a camera FRAME from each slide header, render each canvas once at rest, and a dimmed copy for steps (a) and (c);
  • make every camera move itself, as a CSS transform over the canvas still, so any two slides of a canvas are joined by one move; each slide's media then hold only its own step (d) and its loop (e);
  • replace the hop-by-hop travel: keys pressed in a burst set a destination, and the stage makes one direct move to it (zoom out a little, travel, zoom in), with the bar showing the destination while keys are pressed;
  • cross between canvases by zoom out, cross-fade, zoom in; play ← as the reverse of →;
  • keep ?reduced=1 as cuts between rest stills, and drop any click hook.

Frame

The talk shows the work, not a paper: that it was published is said only on slide 21. Every worked example is a real case from the traces (lab S3, r2c2/2026-v1/traces/, the per-pass test_traces.jsonl behind the submitted runs), chosen per slide to make its mechanism visible; nothing is invented, neither question, query, turn nor answer. The cases were found and checked against the raw traces in EXAMPLES.md, and the author accepted the picks on 2 November 2026 ("go on"); each slide's Example line names its case and cites its material as "EXAMPLES.md, slide N.k", from which quotes and numbers are taken verbatim. Passes are numbered 1 to 4 on the slides (EXAMPLES.md counts some from 0). The Hannibal question stays only where the paper itself uses it (slide 14, and slide 3's card for the simple type). Every number is from papers/r2c2/ or EXAMPLES.md; the questions on slide 3 are the organisers' (results/r2c2topics-and-goldanswers.xlsx), and a question's type, wherever a slide shows one, is the organisers' (results/OfficialResults/r2c2questiontypes), never the trace's own label. No slide counts passes per question as "three or four": the runs use up to four passes (the answer runs pool the first four); 8 questions have extra scratch passes in the trace folders.
