End states and navigation

EPFL deck · a draft for your validation, 4 November 2026 · nothing in the deck has been changed

What you asked. “(1) end of an animation for each concept [...] some of them will loop, some other will loop only the end part, some other will finish with a different animation or even stay still (2) the design of the <- going back [...] we don't want the animation to take the same time when we want it to disappear.” And for the title: “the text cursor staying at the end of jknafou.”

In short. Five end states, chosen by what the slide shows: a result rests (STILL), a ruling ends on its mark (FINALE), something waiting moves a little (IDLE), a running process replays its last beat (LOOP-TAIL), and nothing replays whole. Going back takes half a second and lands at rest; several presses make one move; forward always plays the target's entry at its designed speed.

1 · End states

STILL

rests on its last frame; nothing moves.

When: The slide ends on a result: a number, a ranking, a list, a table, a graph, a code to scan. The default: anything not named below is STILL.

FINALE

the entry's last beat is a closing gesture of another kind (a mark written, a winner swept, a card folded away), then STILL.

When: Something was judged or decided on this slide. The gesture is the full stop and is never replayed.

IDLE

a small motion over the rest that changes no word, no value and no verdict, and never ends: a caret blinks, a “?” breathes, a bead runs an arrow.

When: Something on the slide waits: a handle waits to be typed (its caret), a question is still open (its “?”), a flow keeps carrying things (its bead).

LOOP-TAIL

only the entry's last beat replays, from the rest back to the rest, after a pause.

When: The slide shows a process that runs again and again, and its last beat is that process: the ball's roll through the pipeline.

LOOP-ALL

the whole entry replays.

When: Never on a slide of this talk: an entry tells its story once and the speaker talks over its rest. Kept for the specimen page (and a pre-talk holding screen, if one is ever wanted).

Rules every end state obeys

  • One mover at rest per slide. At most one IDLE or LOOP-TAIL runs on a slide's rest; everything else on it is STILL. Where several concepts could move, the one the slide is about wins (its Communicates).
  • Role decides, not kind. A concept takes the end below only where it is the slide's focus. Brought over from an earlier slide, dimmed or set aside, it is STILL.
  • Rest is a frame of the loop. Every IDLE and LOOP-TAIL starts and ends exactly on the rest still (make_media's seam check, ≥ 32 dB), so a still taken at any moment says the same thing. The caret's still shows it on.
  • Slow and calm. An idle's period is at least 1 s: caret 0.53 s on, 0.53 s off (a text caret's own rate); a “?” breathes in 2.4 s (scale 1 → 1.05 → 1); a bead runs an arrow at A.pulse's pace with at least 0.6 s between beads; a LOOP-TAIL waits 0.8 s at rest before it replays.
  • Rest never changes information. A loop moves only ink that is not data: a caret, a bead, the ball. A number never counts again, a gauge never hunts again, a mark is never rewritten at rest (the one exception, slide 20's gauge, is a gauge with no value: its “?” is the point).

3 · Navigation

Words.

  • entry: the slide's own animation, (a)-(d), at its designed speed (1×).
  • rest: the slide's last frame with its end behaviour running.
  • unwind: a short way back, at most 0.5 s, from wherever the picture is to the previous slide's rest.
  • burst: presses each within 0.5 s of the one before. Every press is acted on at once; a press inside the window only moves the destination, which the bar shows (“→ 7 · Passes 3 and 4”).
  1. → at rest. Slide k+1's entry plays at 1×. A stand-alone or cross-canvas slide first leaves k by a 0.35 s fade of the whole picture (today's exit); a slide that continues k starts on k's rest with no exit, its step (a) dimming what it sets aside (0.5 s). k's idle or loop stops on the spot (120 ms cross-fade, as today).
  2. → during an entry finishes it, and does not advance. If what remains is ≤ 1.8 s it plays at 3× (≤ 0.6 s); if more, the picture cross-fades to the slide's rest in 0.3 s. The end behaviour starts. The next → advances (rule 1). The one choice for you: today every press advances; this rule spends the first press on finishing, as a clicker does in PowerPoint, so a press made a little early never loses a slide.
  3. → × N in a burst: one direct move to k+N. No intermediate entry. The entry begun by the first press (under 0.5 s old) is cut. On one canvas: the camera zooms out about 8 %, travels and zooms in, 0.8 s in all, onto the target's starting frame; across canvases: a 0.35 s cross-fade to it. Then the target's entry plays at 1×. Until the CSS camera exists, every direct move is the 0.35 s cross-fade.
  4. ← at rest: a short unwind, then the previous slide at rest. ≤ 0.5 s, not the reverse of the entry. On a canvas (k continues k−1): what k added goes out in reverse layer order, overlapping: hand marks 0–0.1 s, marker bands and gauges 0.1–0.25 s, cards, words and numbers fade 0.15–0.4 s, lines retract 0.15–0.4 s, while the camera returns to k−1's frame in 0.45 s (ease in-out). Stand-alone or across canvases: a 0.35 s cross-fade from k's rest to k−1's. Then k−1 is at rest and its end behaviour restarts from frame 0 (its loop from the start, the caret on). Nothing is un-typed, un-counted, un-flipped or rolled back: words and numbers fade.
  5. ← during an entry: stop and unwind from there. The entry halts on its current frame, which cross-fades to k−1's rest in 0.35 s. A press back means “leave”, not “show it reversed”.
  6. ← × N in a burst: one move to k−N, at rest. One 0.35 s cross-fade (on one canvas, the camera's direct move, ≤ 0.8 s) to slide k−N's rest. No entry plays, none in between.
  7. Direction decides how you arrive. Forward arrives with the entry, backward arrives at rest. So Home and T go to slide 1 at rest (0.35 s fade); End plays slide 21's entry; a part key (1-9) plays the entry of the part's first slide if it lies ahead, and lands at its rest if it lies behind. A mixed burst (→ → ←) goes by its net count; net zero keeps the current slide (an entry it cut finishes as in rule 2).
  8. A slide already played arrives at rest. Going forward again onto a slide whose entry played in this session (← then →) cross-fades to its rest in 0.35 s: the audience has seen it. New key R: replays the current slide's entry from its start at 1×, for “let me show that again”.
  9. Esc. Cancels a destination that has not started moving; a move already under way lands where it was going.
  10. Reduced motion (?reduced=1 or the OS setting). Cuts between rest stills, as today; no idle runs (the caret is shown on, still).

  2. ← becomes a CSS cross-fade; bw-*.mp4 go.
